✦ Our story

Why we started reading out loud again.

Share the Page is the founding chapter of On the Same Page, a growing network of reading circles working to put the joy back into reading — and to put books within reach of every community.

I. Our mission

Bringing back the joy of reading

For many of us, college has meant trading the lively books that used to fuel our imaginations for dense academic texts — and that shift has quietly pushed a lot of students away from reading for pleasure at all. On the Same Page exists to reverse that. We want reading to feel like something to look forward to again, not another assignment.

Research on representation in literature shows that books can act as mirrors, windows, and sliding glass doors: they reflect readers back to themselves, let them look into lives unlike their own, and — at their best — let them step through into another person's experience entirely. For many people, that's how they first "meet" a culture, a race, or a gender different from their own. Stories are how empathy gets built.

Every chapter of On the Same Page gives its members a way to travel outside their own community through books — to learn from people who live differently but feel familiar things. Meetings are built as a safe space to talk about what actually matters to members right now, using the book as the way in. It's reading as something you look forward to, not something you're assigned.

II. Why representation matters

Mirrors, windows, and sliding glass doors

This idea comes from literacy scholar Dr. Rudine Sims Bishop, and it's the lens we choose books through.

Mirrors

Books that reflect a reader's own life back at them — proof that their experience belongs on the page.

Windows

Books that offer a clear view into a life unlike the reader's own, without asking anything of the person on the other side.

Sliding glass doors

Books immersive enough that a reader doesn't just look in — they step through and come out having felt it.

— based on Dr. Rudine Sims Bishop's 1990 essay on representation in children's and young adult literature

III. Growing chapters

One chapter, becoming many

Share the Page started as a single circle of neighbors. The goal now is for On the Same Page to take root at other schools and organizations, each one run locally but sharing the same mission and format.

IV. Community service

Building libraries in literary deserts

Beyond running chapters, On the Same Page is working toward nonprofit status to fund something bigger: physical libraries in literary deserts, communities where access to books is limited or nonexistent. Every chapter's fundraising contributes to this fund.
